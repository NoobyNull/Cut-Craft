# Toolbars of Your Own

Put the tools you reach for together on a **toolbar of your own**: single tools, whole
tool groups, or both, in the order you like. Make as many as you want, say one for signs
and one for boxes, and name each. [Favourites](help:favourites) is the first of them,
there from the start: it can be renamed, moved and snapped like the rest, but never
deleted.

## Making a toolbar

1. **Right-click** any tool's button or row, in any layout.
2. Pick **Add to New Toolbar**. It is called **My Tools** (then My Tools 2, and so on) and
   floats over the drawing at once.

To add more, right-click another tool and pick **Add to** and the toolbar's name, say **Add
to My Tools**. On a tool already on that toolbar it says **Remove from My Tools**. With more
than six toolbars they are listed under **Add to Toolbar ▸** instead, or always, if
**Options ▸ General ▸ Add to menus** says so.

To put a **whole group** on a toolbar, right-click the group instead: its icon in the Rail
or the Canvas palette, its name under its icons in the Ribbon, or its heading in Classic.
On the toolbar the group is one button that opens its list of tools. It opens when you
point at it, or when you click it if **Options ▸ General ▸ Canvas tool lists open** is set
to On Click.

## Where they show

A new toolbar **floats over the drawing**, near its top left. Every toolbar has a **grip**,
a row of dots at its start with no words: point at it to see the toolbar's name.

- **Drag the dots** to move a floating toolbar anywhere. It keeps its distance from the
  window corner it is nearest, so it stays in place when the window changes size. Dragging
  one of its tools never moves the toolbar.
- **Right-click the dots** for the toolbar's menu:
  - **Snap to Top**, **Bottom**, **Left** or **Right** sticks it to that side of the
    drawing. A snapped toolbar stays there however the window changes, and a drag never
    pulls it off: only this menu does. Toolbars on the top or the foot run across; on the
    left or right they run down.
  - **Float** lets it float again.
  - **Lock Position** (floating only) makes a drag on its grip leave it where it is.
  - **Turn** (floating only) makes it run down instead of across, or back.
  - **Attach to Header** puts it in the layout instead, and **Detach** takes it out again
    (the menu says **Attach to Rail** in Rail, **Attach to Design Tab** in Classic).
  - **Rename Toolbar** and **Delete Toolbar**.

**Options ▸ General ▸ Bars snapped to a side** picks what a snapped toolbar does to the
drawing: **Push the drawing over** (it makes room, so nothing is hidden) or **Over the
drawing** (the toolbar lies over the drawing's edge and nothing changes size). Over the
drawing, a toolbar stays under the view's own bar (2D / 3D, sheet, snapping), and toolbars
on sides next to each other never lie on top of one another.
**Options ▸ General ▸ New toolbars start** picks whether a new toolbar floats or goes in the
layout.

Attached to the layout, a toolbar shows in every [window layout](help:interface_layout):

- **Ribbon** and **Canvas**: a tab of its own after the built-in tabs.
- **Rail**: a button of its own after the **★** button; it opens the toolbar as a list.
- **Classic**: a section of its own at the foot of the **Design** tab.

Right-click its tab, Rail button or Classic heading for the same menu.

## Changing a toolbar

**View ▸ Customize Toolbars...** (also at the foot of every toolbar's menu) shows every tool
beside every toolbar, with buttons to add, take off, move, make, rename and delete. See
[Customize Toolbars](help:customize_toolbars). Or, on the toolbar itself:


- **Drag** an item along its toolbar to move it; a line shows where it will go.
- Or right-click an item and pick **Move Earlier** or **Move Later**.
- **Remove from Toolbar** takes an item off.
- Right-click the toolbar's grip (or its name in the layout) to **Rename Toolbar** or
  **Delete Toolbar**.

Your toolbars are kept with your other settings, the same in every layout.
